> Judge: The story's biggest strength is its gentle, playful search and the warm bond between Alice and Bob, with humorous details that keep a young listener engaged. It would be improved slightly by setting up how the bell ended up in the boot, so Bob's clever hiding feels more natural.

Alice had a best friend with four soft paws, a tail shaped like a question mark, and a tiny brass bell on a blue ribbon collar. His name was Bob, and he was a cat. Every time Bob walked, the bell said ting-a-ling, so Alice always knew where he was.

Every morning they sat together on the back step and counted the birds on the fence. Bob always counted wrong. "One, two, one very fat pigeon," said Alice. Bob blinked his amber eyes, as if that were exactly right.

One Tuesday, Alice noticed something was missing. There was no ting. She looked down. Bob's blue ribbon was still tied around his neck, but the bell was gone.

"Bob!" she gasped. "Where is your bell?"

Bob gave a small, worried mew.

They searched the garden first. They looked under the rhubarb leaves, which were as big as umbrellas. They peeked behind the watering can, which went bonk when Alice knocked it. Bob poked his nose into the hedge and sneezed, a tiny, surprised pfft.

"Listen," whispered Alice, holding up one finger. "We have to listen for it."

They stood very still. A bee buzzed past. The gate creaked. Far away, a dog barked twice. No ting.

They searched the kitchen next. They looked under the table, behind the bread bin, and inside the big yellow colander. Bob checked the laundry basket and pawed at a mountain of socks until a green one flew off and landed on Mum's head.

"Goodness," said Mum, laughing. "Is that a hat now?"

But there was still no bell.

Alice sat on the bottom stair and thought hard. Bob sat beside her and licked one paw. Then she noticed where he kept looking: the boot tray by the front door, where her wellies stood in a row. Bob's gaze kept drifting to the muddy purple boot on the left, and every time it did, his tail gave a little twitch.

"Is it in there?" Alice asked.

Bob stood up, stretched, and padded over to the boot. He sat down beside it and purred.

Alice tipped the boot upside down. Out tumbled a crumbly leaf, a red button, and a fat grey snail that waved its horns at her. Then, with a soft rattle, something small and round rolled out onto the mat.

Ting!

"You clever cat," Alice whispered. "You hid it somewhere safe." The bell was dusty but whole. Alice tied it back onto the blue ribbon. Bob shook his head, and it went ting-a-ling all the way down the hall.

That evening the house grew quiet. The kitchen clock ticked slowly. Alice pulled on her pyjamas and climbed into bed, and Bob hopped up after her, turning around three times before settling on her feet. The lamp dimmed to a soft yellow glow.

"Goodnight, Bob," Alice murmured.

Bob yawned a wide pink yawn. His bell gave one small, sleepy ting.

And then the room was still, and Alice closed her eyes, and so did Bob.

Ting... goodnight.

"Ember!" cheered Dorian. "You're flying!"

## fridge-party (controls)

At midnight, when the house was as quiet as a sleepy sock, Wilbur tiptoed into the kitchen for a drink of water. The fridge gave a long, happy hum. Then it gave a giggle. Wilbur pressed his ear to the cold white door and heard a sound: boing, boing, bip-bop-bip.

"Hello?" he whispered.

The door creaked open a crack, and a warm yellow light spilled across the floor. Wilbur peeked inside. There was a party going on! A tomato wearing a bow tie was bouncing on the butter dish. Two peas were doing the twist. A row of carrot sticks tap-danced across the cheese, going crunch, crunch, crunch.

"Welcome, Wilbur!" squeaked a leek wearing a paper crown. "I am Sorrel, and I am the host."

"Why is everybody in the fridge?" asked Wilbur.

"Because it is the coolest place to party," said Sorrel, "and because our guest of honor has gone missing!"

"Who is the guest of honor?"

"Bodhi the Brussels sprout!" wailed a cucumber, dropping a streamer. "He was rolling round and round the dance floor, and then, plip! He rolled right out through the gap at the bottom."

Wilbur knelt down and peered under the fridge. It was dark and dusty down there. He saw a sock, a purple crayon, and one very surprised dust bunny. No Bodhi.

"Bodhi?" he called. "Are you down there?"

From far under the fridge came a tiny voice. "Boo-oo-oing! It's dark down here, and I'm all squashed flat!"

Wilbur jumped up and searched the drawer. He found a long wooden spoon, a slice of cheese, and a jar lid. "Hold on, Bodhi," he said. "We are coming."

The peas rolled over to help. They pushed the jar lid under the fridge like a little shovel, while the carrot sticks held the cheese steady. Wilbur slid the wooden spoon into the dusty gap and gently tapped. Tap. Tap. Tap. A small green ball bumped against the spoon.

"Oh, that tickles!" giggled Bodhi.

"Hop onto the cheese," said Wilbur. "Slowly, slowly."

Bodhi hopped. The cheese wobbled. Wilbur lifted the spoon, and out rolled Bodhi, round and bouncy and a little bit dusty.

"BOING!" cheered the whole party. The tomato spun three times. The cucumber threw confetti made of grated carrot. Even the butter dish did a wobbly wiggle.

"Hooray for Wilbur!" shouted Sorrel. "Now the party can begin properly. Dance with us!"

Wilbur's toes were cold, but his heart was warm. He tapped his feet with the carrot sticks. He spun with the peas. He slipped on a grape, landed on a pile of lettuce, and laughed so hard that a sleepy yawn escaped him. The vegetables laughed too, a crunchy, crackly, delicious laugh.

But the clock in the hall began to chime. Dong. Dong. Dong. Wilbur looked at the fridge light and then at the dark stairs. He felt a little bit sad to leave, and a little bit proud, and a little bit tired all at once.

"Shh," said Sorrel, very gently. "The party is getting sleepy now."

The music slowed to a soft tinkle, like a spoon tapping a teacup. The tomato stopped bouncing and rested his bow tie on the butter. The peas leaned against each other with tiny, contented sighs. Bodhi curled up in a nest of lettuce leaves, his green cheeks glowing like a night-light.

Wilbur reached out and touched the cold shelf. "Goodnight, Bodhi," he whispered. "Goodnight, Sorrel. Goodnight, everyone."

"Goodnight, Wilbur," the vegetables murmured, drowsy and soft.

The fridge door eased closed with a gentle click. The hum grew low and quiet. Wilbur tiptoed up the stairs, past the sleepy sock and the creaking step, and climbed into bed. Somewhere below, a tiny voice said, "Boing," very softly, and then everything was still.

Sleep now, little one. The fridge is dreaming, and so are the peas.
