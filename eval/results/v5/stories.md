Alice had a best friend with whiskers as long as paintbrushes and a tail that curled like a question mark. His name was Bob, and Bob was a cat.

Every morning, Bob wore a silver bell on a red ribbon around his neck. When he trotted across the kitchen floor, it went ding, ding, ding, like a tiny bird singing. Alice loved that sound more than almost anything.

One sunny Saturday, the bell was gone. Bob searched under the table. He searched behind the fridge. He even poked his nose into the sugar bowl, which made him sneeze. "Achoo!" said Bob, and Alice giggled.

"Ding," whispered Bob, very softly.

Goodnight, Bob.

Clink!

The crickets began to sing. The porch light hummed, soft and low. Leo yawned, and the map slipped gently into his lap.

The night grew quiet. The stars came out, one by one.

Good night, little pirate.

"Flap, flap, wait and see," her gran would say, with a gentle puff of smoke. "Your wings know more than you think."

But Ember did not believe her. Her wings felt like wet paper. Her tummy felt like a bouncing ball.

Her best friends were already up there. Pip the hare zipped between the clouds like a leaf. Lulu the owl swooped in loops and called, "Come on, Ember! It's easy!"

It did not look easy. It looked very high.

"Then we will go slowly," said Gran. "One small step at a time. Or one small flap."

The wind blew slow now. Hush, it whispered. Hush.

One by one, the Moon Flowers folded their petals closed. Pop. Pop. Soft as snow.

Ember's wings rested by her sides, warm and sleepy. Flap, they seemed to say, very softly. Flap.

"Goodnight, little wings," Ember whispered.
