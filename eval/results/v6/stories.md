## alice-bob (normal)

**Request:** A story about a girl named Alice and her best friend Bob, who happens to be a cat.

**Status:** ok (expected ok) · category: friendship
- note: I made Alice the hero and gave Bob a playful trait, a sneeze that comes when he gets excited, since the request didn't say more about them.
- note: I chose a missing moon-shaped bread as the small puzzle, keeping the stakes low and the pace gentle.

### Writer only (no judge): *Alice, Bob, and the Missing Moon Bread*
mean 4.36, min 4, PASS · 621 words (target 600) · grade 3.7

> Judge: The story has a warm, funny voice, a memorable opening, and a cozy bedtime ending that settles gently. Its most important fix is to make the best-friend bond between Alice and Bob more explicit through a moment that shows their closeness.

Bob was a ginger cat with four white socks and a sneeze that came out like a tiny trumpet. It arrived whenever he got excited. So when Alice padded into the kitchen one grey Saturday morning and found him nose-deep in the bread bin, she knew something was up.

"Bob," she whispered, "where is the moon bread?"

Grandma Rose had baked a crescent loaf that morning, golden and crackly, with a dusting of sugar on top. It had been cooling on the windowsill. Now the windowsill was empty, except for a few flakes of crust and one very guilty whisker.

Bob's ears flattened. "Pffft-CHOO!" he said. It was not an answer, but it was a very loud one.

Alice looked under the table. She looked behind the flour jar and inside the teapot. She even peeked into the washing basket, where a sock was snoring softly. No moon bread. Only a trail of golden crumbs leading out through the open back door, like a path of tiny stars.

"Follow the crumbs," Alice said. Bob's tail curled up like a question mark, and off they went.

The crumbs led down the garden path, past the wobbly bean poles and a dented watering can. At the bottom step sat a plump grey pigeon, pecking happily at the very middle of the moon.

Bob crouched low. His whiskers quivered. His nose wrinkled. Alice could feel a sneeze building up inside him, like a kettle about to whistle.

"Don't," Alice breathed. "Stay very still."

Bob pressed his lips together so hard that his face went squashed and funny. The sneeze grumbled and fizzed somewhere deep inside him.

Alice thought hard. A pigeon was not scared of a cat. A pigeon was only scared of sudden noises and quick feet. She pulled a heel of plain bread from her pocket, the crust she had been saving for the ducks. She crumbled it onto the path, one piece at a time, far from the step.

"Coo, coo," she murmured, sounding as much like a pigeon as she could.

The pigeon cocked its head. It hopped off the step. It hopped toward the crumbs. Plip, plop, plip. Bob's eyes went very wide. He crept forward one paw at a time, and when the pigeon was busy gobbling, Alice gently lifted the moon bread into her apron.

Then the sneeze escaped. "PFFFT-CHOOOO!"

The pigeon flapped away with a squawk, leaving behind one fat grey feather and a very surprised cat.

Alice laughed until she had to sit on the step. Bob sat beside her, looking proud and sorry all at once.

"Well," said Grandma Rose from the back doorway, drying her hands on her apron. She had heard every squawk. "A moon bread with a nibble taken out of it is still a moon bread, I suppose. And I have enough flour for another."

So they baked a second moon together. Alice shaped the dough into a crescent while Bob sat on a stool and kept his nose well away from the flour bag. He did not manage it. A puff of white drifted up like a little snowstorm, and Grandma said it was the finest snow she had seen all year.

That evening, when the sky had turned the color of plums, Alice curled up in bed. Bob settled at her feet with a rumbly purr. On the windowsill, the new moon bread sat cooling, a pale golden crescent against the dark glass.

Outside, the garden was still. The pigeon was asleep in the apple tree. The watering can had stopped dripping. The house grew quiet, and the quiet grew soft.

"Goodnight, Bob," Alice whispered.

Bob's purr went on and on, slow as a sleepy clock.

"Goodnight, moon."

Outside, the stars blinked on, one by one. The crickets hummed softer now, and the oak tree whispered in the breeze. Rocket curled up at the foot of the bed with a long, happy sigh. Leo's eyelids grew heavy, and his yawn stretched wide.

Goodnight, little captain.
