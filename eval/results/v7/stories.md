## alice-bob (normal)

**Request:** A story about a girl named Alice and her best friend Bob, who happens to be a cat.

**Status:** ok (expected ok) · category: friendship
- note: I made Alice the hero and gave Bob a curious, whisker-loving personality, since the request didn't describe them.
- note: I chose a gentle mystery about dim stars as the central idea, with a small helper moth to keep the problem low-stakes.

### Writer only (no judge): *Alice, Bob, and the Dim Little Star*
mean 4.29, min 4, PASS · 537 words (target 600) · grade 3.5

> Judge: The story's biggest strength is its cozy, sensory wind-down, with Bob's purr and the teakettle image carrying the bedtime mood. Its most important fix is to give Bob one concrete action in the cleaning scene so he is more than a watcher in his own friendship story.

Alice lived in a yellow house at the end of Pebble Lane. Her best friend was a ginger cat named Bob, who had a crooked tail, a purr like a teakettle, and whiskers that could sniff out a sunbeam from across the room.

One evening, Alice climbed onto the windowsill with Bob tucked under her arm. "Look, Bob," she whispered. "The little star above the apple tree is dim." Bob blinked his slow cat blink. Every night that star twinkled like a silver button. Tonight it looked fuzzy, as if someone had breathed on it.

Bob's ears swiveled toward the glass. "Mrrp?" he said, which in cat means, Tell me more.

Then a moth fluttered out of the dark and bumped against the window. Thump. Flutter. Thump. It was a plump little moth with dusty gray wings and a furry body, and it would not stop knocking.

The moth flew down to a corner of the glass and landed on a shiny smudge. Alice pressed her nose close. The smudge was sticky, and it was shaped like a small handprint, right in front of the star.

Alice giggled. "Somebody had jammy fingers," she said. "I bet it was Sam."

Alice rubbed the smudge with her sleeve. Squeak, squish. It smeared into a long, wobbly streak, and the star looked even dimmer. Bob patted at the glass with one paw, then sat back and shook his foot. "Mrrrp!" His paw was sticky now, too, and he gave it a look of great disgust.

The moth bumped the glass again, softly, as if to say, Slow down. Alice took a deep breath. "Grandma knows all about smudges," she whispered, and she tiptoed down the hall in her socks.

Grandma was knitting by the fire with her glasses on her nose. When she heard the story, she smiled and fetched a bowl of warm water and a soft cloth. "Small circles, not big rubs," she said. "Smudges like gentle hands."

Alice dipped the cloth and began wiping in tiny circles. Round and round, soft as a whisper. Bob sat on the sill and watched every circle with his tail wrapped around his feet. The moth landed on Alice's knuckle and stayed there, as still as a button.

Little by little, the sticky smudge loosened and slid away. Alice rinsed the cloth and wiped one last time. The glass was clear and cool under her fingers.

Above the apple tree, the little star blinked. Then it glowed, bright and silver, like it had just woken from a nap.

"We did it!" Alice gasped. Bob gave a proud little chirrup and flicked his crooked tail. The moth lifted off Alice's hand and circled the star once, twice, before it drifted away into the cool night.

Grandma tucked the cloth away and yawned a big, slow yawn. Alice climbed into bed, and Bob curled up on her pillow, purring softer and softer. The star twinkled, slow and sleepy now. Its light crept across the floor and over the quilt, warm as honey.

"Goodnight, little star," Alice whispered.

Bob's purr hummed like a teakettle at rest.

Goodnight, Bob. Goodnight, moth.

The room grew quiet. Then very quiet. And then the whole house was still.
